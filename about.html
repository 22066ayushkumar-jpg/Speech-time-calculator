What is Speech Time Calculator?

Speech Time Calculator is a free online speaking time estimator. Paste your script or enter a word count to instantly see how long your words will take to say out loud — and how long they take to read silently. It is built for students, teachers, presenters, podcasters, voice-over artists, and anyone who needs a reliable words to minutes converter.

What you can do with it

  • Estimate speaking time and silent reading time from any script.
  • Choose a scenario — TED Talk, presentation, lecture, toast, storytelling, pitch, or voice-over — or set a custom pace from 60 to 400 words per minute.
  • Set a time limit and get your exact word budget, with under, optimal, and over-target guidance.
  • Rehearse with a built-in practice timer, live pace tracking, and a distraction-free teleprompter.
  • Copy a time summary or download your script with estimated timestamps.
  • Work in character mode for Chinese, Japanese, and Korean scripts.

Private by design

Every calculation happens in your browser. Your text never leaves your device, is never uploaded to a server, and no account or sign-up is required. Drafts are saved only in your own browser's local storage so you can pick up where you left off.

How Our Speech Time Benchmarks Are Verified

Speech time is calculated with a transparent formula: minutes = words ÷ words per minute (WPM), then converted to minutes and seconds and rounded to the nearest second. The same method powers the reference matrix on the homepage and the live estimate in the calculator.

Why 130 WPM is the default

130 WPM is the midpoint of the 100–160 WPM reference band used for public speaking planning. It represents an average conversational presentation pace: clear enough for an audience to follow, but not so slow that a talk loses momentum. The calculator also offers 100 WPM (slow), 160 WPM (fast), and custom rates from 60 to 400 WPM so you can model your own delivery instead of relying on a single average.

Presentation pacing vs. silent reading

Toastmasters-style speeches and TED-style talks commonly land around 130–150 WPM, with deliberate pauses for emphasis, transitions, and audience response. Silent reading is much faster: this tool uses 230 WPM for reading time, which is why the silent-reading column is shorter than the spoken columns for the same word count. Reading estimates are for comprehension and study planning, not for rehearsal pacing.

Character-per-minute (CPM) for Chinese, Japanese, and Korean

Word boundaries differ across CJK writing systems, so word-per-minute maths is unreliable for those scripts. In CPM mode the tool counts CJK characters and divides by a character-per-minute rate: 300 CPM for Chinese and Japanese, and 350 CPM for Korean. The result is a character-based speaking estimate that can be compared directly with the spoken-time figures.

Editorial standards

Rates are presented as planning estimates, not guarantees. Actual delivery varies with pauses, emphasis, audience interaction, slide changes, and language. The reference tables are reviewed against standard rhetoric guidance and the 100–160 WPM speaking-rate benchmark band, alongside Toastmasters timing guidelines and the acoustic benchmarks used in professional speech work. All calculations run locally in your browser, so scripts are never uploaded or stored on a server.
