How Many Words is a 3-Minute Speech?

Speech Time Calculator · Free to use · 100% private · No sign-ups

The short answer

A 3-minute speech is approximately 390 words at a natural conversational pace of 130 words per minute (WPM). If you are giving a slow, emotional wedding toast with pauses for laughter (100 WPM), aim for 300 words. For a fast, energetic pitch (160 WPM), write up to 480 words.

That puts your realistic 3-minute range between 300 and 480 words. Your pace, your nerves, and how often the audience reacts matter more than the average — so measure your own delivery with the speech time calculator before you lock the script.

How to structure a 3-minute talk (by scenario)

The 3-Minute Thesis (3MT) & academic pitch — 330–360 words

University 3MT competitions time you at exactly 3:00 and disqualify overruns, so write to the 115 WPM deliberate pace and keep 330–360 words on the page. Use one static slide — every click steals 4–6 seconds and pulls your eye off the audience. After your hook, hold a 3-second pause: it lets the problem statement land, resets your breathing, and is the single cheapest way to stop the adrenaline-driven rush that makes academic speakers hit 140+ WPM and run out of time before their methods point.

Wedding toasts & best man speeches — 280–320 words

A toast is delivered at roughly 100 WPM because you are emotional, making eye contact, and waiting for reactions. Budget for the audience: laughter consumes 30–40 seconds of a 3-minute slot — one good joke, one round of "aww" at the photo, and one applause beat after "raise your glasses" can eat half your time. Write 280–320 words, mark each laugh line with a pause cue, and you will finish warm instead of hurried.

The 3-minute presentation slide rule

Apply the 1-slide-per-minute rule: a 3-minute slot supports a maximum of 3–4 slides (title/hook, two core points, takeaway). More slides than minutes forces you to narrate visually — average slide dwell time drops below 40 seconds, retention collapses, and the deck, not you, ends up pacing the talk. Build your word budget around 3 slides at 330–390 words and rehearse with the transitions counted in.

3-minute speech FAQs

How many pages is a 3-minute speech?

A 390-word script at the average 130 WPM pace is about 0.8 single-spaced pages or 1.5 to 1.6 double-spaced pages, set in 12 pt Arial or Times New Roman with 1-inch margins.

How many paragraphs is a 3-minute speech?

Typically 3 to 5 paragraphs: a 65-word intro, two 130-word body points, and a 65-word conclusion. That structure lands right on the 390-word budget at 130 words per minute.

How many slides do I need for a 3-minute presentation?

3 to 4 slides maximum — 1 title or hook slide, 2 core point slides, and 1 takeaway slide. Using the 1-slide-per-minute rule keeps your visuals in step with your script instead of rushing you off the stage.

Is 400 words too long for a 3-minute speech?

At 130 WPM, 400 words takes 3 minutes and 5 seconds without a single pause. If your delivery includes slides, applause, or audience reactions, trim it to 330–350 words so it still lands inside the slot.
