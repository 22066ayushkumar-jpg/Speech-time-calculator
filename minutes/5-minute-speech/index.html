How Many Words is a 5-Minute Speech?

Speech Time Calculator · Free to use · 100% private · No sign-ups

The short answer

The arithmetic is simple and worth memorising: words ÷ 130 = minutes. A 5-minute slot therefore holds 650 words at the average business pace. The same 5 minutes stretches from 500 words at a measured 100 WPM toast pace to 800 words at a fast 160 WPM pitch — a 300-word swing that is entirely about delivery, not content.

In manuscript terms that is 1.3 single-spaced pages, or 2.6 double-spaced, which is the practical way to judge a draft you have already written. Aim for 553 words instead of the full 650, and the 85% you leave on the table absorbs the pause after your opening line, the laugh you did not script, and the slide that loads a beat late.

How Many Pages & Paragraphs is a 5-Minute Speech?

Structure for a class presentation, a lightning talk, or a five-minute conference slot

Open with the claim in one sentence, spend the middle on a single piece of evidence, and close on the action you want. For a class presentation, a lightning talk, or a five-minute conference slot, that shape is about 78 words of opening, 455 of body, and 117 of close. Mark a deliberate pause after the opening line — it is the cheapest way to stop the adrenaline rush that makes speakers hit 150+ WPM and run out of slot before their last point.

Pages, paragraphs and slides for 5 minutes

At 1.3 single-spaced pages you want roughly 5–6 paragraphs and 5–6 slides. The one-slide-per-45-to-60-seconds rule means a 5-minute talk supports no more than 5–6 slides; beyond that, average dwell time drops under 45 seconds, attention fragments, and the deck ends up pacing you instead of the other way round.

Why trim to 553 words (the 85% rule)

Rehearsed reading is consistently faster than performed delivery. The gap is not nerves alone: it is breathing, a slide change, a laugh, a sip of water, a question called out from the third row. Writing to 85% of your budget — 553 words instead of 650 — buys back about 45 sec of slack, which is the difference between finishing to applause and finishing to a red timer on the lectern.

5-minute speech FAQs

How many words is a 5-minute speech?

About 650 words at 130 words per minute, the pace most presenters settle into when speaking clearly to an audience. Plan 500 words if you speak slowly or expect applause, and up to 800 if you talk quickly.

How many pages is a 5-minute speech?

1.3 single-spaced pages or 2.6 double-spaced pages in 12 pt Arial or Times New Roman with 1-inch margins. That assumes 500 words per single-spaced page and 250 per double-spaced page — the standard manuscript convention.

How long should I rehearse a 5-minute speech?

Read it aloud at least three times, and time every run. Reading silently runs about 230 words per minute, which is roughly 75% faster than speaking, so a silent read-through tells you nothing about your real duration. Aim to land at 4 min 15 sec rather than a full 5 minutes, so you have room for a pause that lands well.

Is a 5-minute speech enough for a class presentation, a lightning talk, or a five-minute conference slot?

Yes. Class Presentation & Lightning Talk is the standard use for this length, and 5 minutes comfortably fits one central claim, one piece of supporting evidence, and a clear close. The failure mode is never too little time; it is trying to make two competing points and landing neither.
